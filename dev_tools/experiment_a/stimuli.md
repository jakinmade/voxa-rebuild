# Condition C — Stimulus-response tasks

Design rule: give the participant something concrete to react to, not
a prompt to perform against. Mirrors the register contrast the current
Screen 3 starters use (professional/defensive vs unfiltered/reactive)
but as "reply to this" rather than "complete this sentence" — the
sentence-starter format is exactly what makes people self-conscious
about being tested.

Instruction shown to the participant, identical framing for both:
"Someone just sent you this. Reply the way you actually would —
real message, not a polished one."

Same word-count floor as production Screen 3: each reply needs to be
at least 10 words to count.

---

## Stimulus 1 — professional/defensive register

> "Hey — client just flagged that the deliverable we sent Friday
> doesn't match what we discussed on the call. Can you take a look
> and let me know what happened?"

This mirrors starter index [0] in the current product (the
defensive-professional register): something that puts the
participant on the spot professionally and pulls out how they
actually explain, own, or push back under mild pressure.

## Stimulus 2 — unfiltered/reactive register

> "Just so you know, the deadline moved up to Friday. Sorry for the
> late notice, need it wrapped by then."

This mirrors starter index [3] (unfiltered-emotional register):
something mildly frustrating and out of their control, pulling out
how they actually react when something lands on them without
warning, not how they'd write it up for an audience.

---

Both are intentionally short and unremarkable — believable as a real
Slack/email message someone would actually get, not a constructed
"test" scenario. If a participant asks "what should I say," the
instruction stands: reply the way you actually would.
